For ethical shopping in Japan

Turn “seems good” into something you can verify.

Plant-based, allergen-free, organic, fair trade, upcycled. The vocabulary has grown quickly; shared definitions have not. Ethical Pantry is an independent reference for people choosing these products in Japan — what each term means, which certifications sit behind it, and how to check a claim yourself.

Start with a category

The terms you meet on packaging and in online shops — definition, certification, and how to verify.

Plant-based

Food made without animal-derived ingredients. It overlaps with “vegan” but the two words do not mean the same thing.

Allergen-free

Food made without specific allergens. Japan's system mandates disclosure when an allergen is present — not a guarantee of absence.

Gluten-free

Food without gluten from wheat, barley or rye. Japan's rice-flour tradition makes this easier than it looks.

No added MSG

Products made without added umami seasonings. Like “additive-free” generally, what it covers varies by product.

Organic

Products made with ingredients from organic agriculture. In Japan the legal backing is the Organic JAS standard.

Fair trade

Goods traded so that producers receive a fair return. Two different certification models exist.

Cruelty-free

Products developed without animal testing. A separate axis from vegan.

Reducing food loss

Efforts to cut the waste of edible food, and the products that come out of them.

Upcycling

Turning material headed for disposal into something of higher value. Distinct from recycling.

Plastic-free

Products made without petroleum-based plastic. Not the same as “biodegradable” or “bio-based”.

Made with welfare workplaces

Goods produced in partnership with workplaces employing people with disabilities.

Small producers

Goods from small-batch makers. Lower volume often means more visible provenance.

Makers

Profiles of companies and brands actually producing in this space.

VEGHEET

Thin sheets made from out-of-spec vegetables, produced by Takeshita Sangyo in Hirado City, Nagasaki.

Fermenstation

A company upcycling unused resources through fermentation, starting with ethanol made from organic rice grown on fallow paddies in Oshu, Iwate.

BEIGIC

A Korean vegan skincare brand built around green coffee bean ingredients. It launched in Japan in 2020.

COCOWELL

An Osaka-based coconut specialist, making everything from food to body care from coconut.

PAPLUS

A plastic alternative made from paper and plant-derived material, developed by Camino Inc.

Little Branch

An Ehime-based brand working with local forest resources — log-grown shiitake and cedar products.
