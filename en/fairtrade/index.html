Fair trade

Goods traded so that producers receive a fair return. Two different certification models exist.

Two approaches

Fair trade works through two broad mechanisms. The first is product certification — the international Fairtrade mark (FLO) being the best known. It covers defined commodities such as coffee, cocoa, sugar, bananas and cotton, and builds a minimum price and a premium into the standard.

The second certifies organisations rather than products. The World Fair Trade Organization (WFTO) verifies that an enterprise as a whole operates on fair trade principles. This works for categories such as handicrafts, where per-commodity standards are hard to write.

Uncertified fair trade

Many businesses trade directly with producers and publish their pricing and relationships without holding certification, which costs money to obtain — smaller operations often skip it. What to look at then is how specifically they disclose the producer, the region and the terms.

What to check

  • Which certification, if any (FLO, WFTO, other)
  • Without certification, whether the producer and terms are disclosed
  • “Fair trade”, “ethical” and “sustainable” are not synonyms
  • For coffee and cocoa, certification plus single-origin information tells you more than either alone
