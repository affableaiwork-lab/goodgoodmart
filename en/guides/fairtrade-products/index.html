How to choose fair trade products

It isn't only coffee and chocolate. The main product categories, and what to check beyond the certification mark.

What counts as a fair trade product

A fair trade product is one made under trading terms that return a fair price to the producer. Both certified goods and uncertified goods that publish their trading terms get called “fair trade” (the certification mechanics themselves are covered in our Fair Trade category).

The main categories

  • Coffee and cocoa: the most widely certified category, with a minimum price and a premium built in
  • Sugar and bananas: commodities covered by the international Fairtrade (FLO) mark
  • Cotton and apparel: often overlaps with organic cotton certification
  • Honey, nuts and spices: many small producers, where certification cost often pushes toward direct trade instead
  • Handicrafts: hard to standardise per product, so organisational certification (WFTO) dominates here

What to check when choosing

  • If there's a certification mark: which one (FLO, WFTO, or other)
  • If there's no certification: whether the producer, region and trading terms are disclosed
  • If the price looks unusually low: whether a premium is realistically reflected in it
  • Fair trade and organic are separate axes — some products satisfy both, many only one

Why certification isn't universal

Certification costs money and paperwork, which is a real burden for small producer cooperatives. Its absence doesn't by itself mean a product isn't fairly traded — how specifically the trading terms are disclosed is the practical substitute for a certification mark.
