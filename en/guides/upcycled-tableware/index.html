Upcycled tableware and tools

Coffee grounds, bamboo powder, rice husk — what to look at in objects made from by-products.

The usual inputs

  • Coffee grounds: post-extraction, generated in volume and reliably
  • Bamboo powder: a by-product of bamboo processing; in Japan it also connects to grove management
  • Rice husk: a by-product of milling, light and hard
  • Wood flour: sawmill offcuts
  • Shell and eggshell: used for their calcium content

The binder decides the product

None of these materials can be moulded on their own; they are mixed with a binder. So a “dish made from coffee grounds” typically contains grounds as a minority fraction, with resin making up the rest.

What that resin is matters. A biodegradable binder makes the whole object potentially degradable; a conventional plastic binder does not. This is usually not visible on the packaging, so the material description on the maker's own site is where to look.

Practical points

  • Heat tolerance — microwave and dishwasher safe or not
  • Odour transfer — coffee-based materials can smell at first
  • Durability and brittleness
  • Which waste category it falls into

Why it is worth it

Upcycled goods usually cost more than the conventional equivalent. What justifies that is turning a disposal cost into value by finding a use for a by-product. If the object is short-lived, the benefit shrinks. Longevity deserves as much attention as price.
