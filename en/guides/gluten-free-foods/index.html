A practical gluten-free food guide

What's actually available at convenience stores and supermarkets, by category, from rice-flour bread to seasonings.

Staples you can choose gluten-free

  • Rice-flour bread: check the ingredients for added wheat gluten
  • Rice noodles and rice vermicelli: noodles made mainly from rice
  • Juwari soba: buckwheat noodles with no wheat binder (“nihachi” soba does contain wheat)
  • Mochi and dango: rice-based snacks

What gluten-free soy sauce (tamari) is

Standard dark soy sauce uses wheat as one of its main ingredients. Tamari is made mainly from soybeans, with little or no wheat. Not every tamari is gluten-free, though — it still needs checking on the ingredients label.

The downsides sometimes raised against gluten-free

  • Diets built around refined rice flour or starch can end up lower in fibre
  • Gluten-free products tend to cost more than their wheat equivalents
  • Restricting gluten without a medical reason can unbalance a diet rather than improve it
  • “Gluten-free equals healthy” is not a claim medicine generally supports

Finding it at convenience stores and supermarkets

Shelves explicitly labelled “gluten-free” are still limited in Japan. Rice crackers, rice-flour confectionery and dried juwari soba are often gluten-free in practice even without the label — reading the ingredients list is what actually widens the options.
