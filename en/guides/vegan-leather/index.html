Understanding vegan leather

“Not animal-derived” and “better for the environment” are separate claims. What the materials actually are.

It is not one material

“Vegan leather” is an umbrella term for leather-like materials made without animal hide. What sits underneath it varies enormously. Broadly, there are three families.

  • Synthetic polymers: polyurethane (PU) and PVC — not animal-derived, but petroleum-derived
  • Plant-derived: cactus, pineapple leaf, apple, mushroom mycelium
  • Recycled: built on substrates such as recycled PET polyester

What is inside “plant leather”

This is the most misunderstood part. Most materials sold as cactus or pineapple leather use plant fibre as a substrate with polyurethane for the surface and binding. The bio-based proportion varies widely between products, and figures well under half are common.

That is not a defect — it is how water resistance and durability are achieved. But it does mean “plant-derived leather” should not be read as “biodegradable”.

Compared with animal leather

Animal leather is durable and, maintained, lasts a long time. Against that sits the environmental burden of tanning. Vegan leather avoids the animal, but synthetic versions are often less durable, and if that raises replacement frequency the total picture shifts.

There is no general answer. It depends on whether your criterion is animal use or total environmental burden.

What to check

  • The composition of both the surface and the substrate
  • The bio-based percentage, and whether it is stated as a number
  • Any statement or warranty about durability
  • Whether it can be repaired
