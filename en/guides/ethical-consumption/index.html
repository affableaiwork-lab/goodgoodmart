What is ethical consumption?

There isn't one definition of “doing it right.” What separates ethical, sustainable and fair trade as terms.

A definition — and who sets it

“Ethical consumption” has no legal definition. Japan's Consumer Affairs Agency describes it loosely as “consumption that considers people, society, communities and the environment, including local revitalisation and employment” — a direction, not a standard. In practice, what counts as ethical is judged by a different yardstick in every category.

How the related terms differ

  • Ethical consumption: the broadest term, covering people, society and environment generally
  • Sustainable: focused mainly on environmental and resource durability
  • Fair trade: narrowly about the terms of trade — a fair return to the producer
  • Organic: about growing or manufacturing method, usually backed by certification

What it looks like in practice

  • Choosing products that disclose their materials and producers
  • Understanding what a certification label actually covers (see our guide to reading ethical labels)
  • Buying only what you need, and using it for longer
  • Buying directly from local producers

A pitfall worth watching for

Because “ethical” is not a regulated term, some claims carry no specific backing behind them. The more useful signal is not the word itself but whether checkable detail — materials, producers, certification — is actually there.
